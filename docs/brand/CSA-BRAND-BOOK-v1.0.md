## 1. Brand foundation

Cyber Security Agency Australia (CSA) is presented as an institutional technology company: serious, controlled, intelligent and distinctly private-sector. The identity must communicate authority without suggesting that CSA is an Australian Government body.

### Brand promise

CSA builds and governs technology intended to be trusted in consequential environments. Its visual system therefore combines institutional discipline with modern cyber and intelligent-systems language.

### Core line

Technology • Governance • Intelligent Systems

### Motto

PROTECT | ENABLE | SECURE | ADVANCE

### Public-facing line

A SAFER DIGITAL TOMORROW

## 2. Corporate Arms

The two-eagle CSA Corporate Arms are the highest-level institutional mark. They are reserved for the institutional entrance, formal identity pages, selected covers, ceremonial applications and controlled seal/stamp contexts.

The Corporate Arms are proprietary private-company identity. They must never be described or presented as the Commonwealth Coat of Arms or as evidence of Australian Government status, sponsorship or endorsement.

## 4. Logo system

The primary logo/wordmark is the everyday corporate mark. Use it in website navigation, proposals, presentations, letterhead, business cards and routine corporate communication. The detailed Arms should not be forced into small spaces.

### Clear space

Maintain clear space around the logo equal to at least the height of the letter C in CSA. Do not allow text, borders, photographs or interface controls to enter this area.

### Minimum size

Digital wordmark: 140 px minimum recommended width. Detailed Corporate Arms: 220 px minimum recommended width. Below this, use the simplified wordmark or star icon.

## 5. Star / favicon system

The star is the compact digital identifier for favicons, app icons and constrained UI. It is not a replacement for the Corporate Arms in formal institutional contexts.

Navy and white are dominant. Royal blue and steel provide technology depth. Silver supports formal applications. Gold is a restrained accent only and must not dominate the identity.

## 7. Typography

Primary digital and document type: Aptos or an equivalent clean modern sans-serif. Formal display applications may use a restrained serif for institutional headings. Avoid decorative sci-fi fonts in official documents.

Recommended hierarchy: H1 32–48 px digital / 24–30 pt print; H2 20–28 px / 16–20 pt; body 16–18 px / 9.5–11 pt; captions 12–14 px / 7.5–9 pt.

## 8. Institutional entrance

The closed entrance is the primary arrival state: dark navy doors, Corporate Arms, company name and a single ENTER CSA action. It should feel private, controlled and premium rather than governmental.

The opening state transitions from dark institutional control into a bright internal environment. The Corporate Arms sit directly ahead. Company, Ecosystem and Contact become the three principal routes.

## 10. Website identity

The top navigation should use the scalable CSA SVG wordmark, not a raster screenshot. The detailed Arms remain the institutional hero mark. The favicon family uses the star device. All identity assets must have meaningful accessible text or be marked decorative where appropriate.

Canonical public domain: https://cs-agency.com.au/
Sitemap: https://cs-agency.com.au/sitemap.xml
Corporate email: info@cs-agency.com.au

Internal links should reinforce the Company, Ecosystem and Contact hierarchy. Temporary deployment addresses are not public canonical destinations.

## 11. Stationery

### Business card

Use the primary logo, navy field and restrained gold accent. Personal details must be factual and current. QR codes should resolve only to controlled CSA destinations.

### Letterhead

Letterhead should remain mostly white, with identity concentrated in the header/footer. The Arms may appear as a small formal mark or low-opacity watermark.

## 13. Seal and stamp

The CSA seal/stamp is a private corporate authenticity device for approved certified-document workflows. It must not imitate or claim the legal character of a government seal, notarial seal or statutory authority.

## 14. Email identity

Email signatures should use the simplified logo, sender name and role, verified contact details, canonical website and restrained social links. Avoid oversized graphics, animated banners and unsupported claims.

## 15. Sitemap and backlinks

The sitemap should expose the institutional structure clearly and consistently. Public backlinks should resolve to the canonical CSA domain. Structured organisation metadata, canonical tags, sitemap entries and internal navigation should use the same naming.

## 16. Accessibility and digital rules

- Provide descriptive alt text for meaningful identity images; decorative images should use empty alt text.
- Maintain WCAG-conscious contrast for text and controls.
- Keep keyboard focus visible and preserve logical tab order.
- Do not encode essential information only in colour.
- Use SVG for the header wordmark and simple marks; use high-resolution raster or professionally redrawn vector artwork for the detailed Arms.
- Do not stretch, skew, rotate or crop the canonical Arms.
- Mobile must preserve the same identity hierarchy as desktop.

## 17. Misuse and legal distinction

- Do not use the Commonwealth Coat of Arms or reproduce its distinctive composition.
- Do not describe CSA as an Australian Government agency unless that legal status changes and is independently established.
- Do not use kangaroo-and-emu supporters as the canonical CSA Arms.
- Do not imply government endorsement, accreditation or statutory powers through visual presentation.
- Do not use third-party logos, names or certification marks without permission.
- Do not make gold the dominant colour; it remains an accent.
- Do not alter the two-eagle supporter requirement without Founder approval.

## 18. Governance and approvals

The Corporate Arms, primary logo, star device, motto, palette, institutional entrance and legal identity block are controlled brand assets. Material changes require Founder approval and a recorded version update. Website changes should be version-controlled, reviewed and verified on desktop and mobile before release.

Brand book owner: Cyber Security Agency Australia Pty Limited. Version 1.0 is the baseline identity standard established from the approved two-eagle design direction.
